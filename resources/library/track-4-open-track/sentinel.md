## Summary

Sentinel is a forecasting organization that describes its purpose as "Identifying global catastrophes in a rapidly-changing world." It publishes periodic risk briefings covering AI, geopolitical, biological, and cyber risk, and promotes a public track record of calibrated probabilistic forecasts. The homepage is a marketing/landing page rather than a repository of reports; it does not itself contain substantive writeups.

## Key Claims / Findings

- Tagline: "Identifying global catastrophes in a rapidly-changing world."
- Publishes a periodic risk publication via a "latest brief," linked out to the organization's blog rather than hosted on the homepage itself.
- States it produces weekly briefings on global risk, covering "AI, geopolitical, bio, cyber, and more."
- Maintains a "Track Record" page/section headlined "World-class forecasting, judged publicly," which displays a calibration chart (predicted probability vs. observed frequency, plotted against number of predictions). The homepage fetch did not surface the underlying numeric data behind this chart.

- Media logos displayed (no specific coverage content shown): a16z, Astral Codex Ten, CNN, Financial Times, TechCrunch, Vox, Wired, Zero Hedge.

- Sign-up call to action: "Sign up to our weekly briefings" / "Curated global risk coverage, weekly."

## Relevance to the sprint

Sentinel is a live example of an organization operationalizing forecasting as a public epistemic product: a calibration-tracked, judged forecasting operation applied specifically to catastrophic and AI-related risk. For Track 4 (Open Track), it is a relevant case study of how forecasting track records are presented as a credibility signal, and of the gap between claiming "world-class, judged publicly" calibration and making the underlying data inspectable from the public-facing page, which is itself a point of interest for research on forecasting adoption and trust.
