## Submission Requirements

- Required: a research report as a **PDF**, using the **official template**:
  https://docs.google.com/document/d/17b3JWki-ZkaqdmqGI5d0gof-HwlGuWU9Mc1_klNZXKs/copy?usp=sharing
- Maximum **8 pages**, not counting references and appendices.
- Project title and abstract, **150–250 words**.
- Author names and affiliations.
- A **"Limitations and Dual-Use / Ethical Considerations"** appendix — required.
- Optional: a public GitHub repo; a 3–5 minute video demo.

### Recommended Report Structure
Introduction, Related Work, Methodology, Results, Discussion, Limitations and Dual-Use / Ethical Considerations (required), References. Page says most strong projects land at 4–8 pages.

### Submission form fields (on the sprint page's "Submit your project")
Required: Project title; Project summary (short abstract — what you did, what you found); PDF report (up to 25 MB); "Are you interested in publishing this project?" (Yes/No); Tracks (choose every track that fits); Team name; Location; Team member 1 name and email.

Optional: Presentation recording; Project code; Slides (PDF/PowerPoint/Keynote/ODP, up to 25 MB); Project image (PNG/JPEG/WebP/GIF, up to 25 MB); Additional material; Discord username and Google Scholar profile per member.

Note: the form itself does not restate the 150–250 word limit for the summary — that requirement is stated on the Guidelines tab.

- Team size: "Solo or team: enter solo or as a team. Teams of up to 5 are recommended; larger groups are allowed." (The Overview tab separately says "Teams of one to five people" — slightly different phrasing, same gist.)
- Projects per person: at most **two** projects per person; each needs its own submission with a unique title.

## Judging Criteria (Rubric)

All projects — regardless of track — are scored on the same three dimensions, 1–5 each. Tracks orient the project but do not affect scoring.

### 1. Impact Potential & Innovation
*How much would this matter if it worked, and how innovative is it?* (For 4–5, judges also ask whether the work is new to the field or replicates recent work.)
- **1 — Negligible**: No clear problem, or no meaningful novelty.
- **2 — Limited**: Real problem, but generic or well-trodden approach; incremental at best.
- **3 — Moderate**: Clear problem and reasonable approach; some novelty beyond routine use of existing tools.
- **4 — Significant**: Important problem with an original approach, or a neglected problem area; others could build on it.
- **5 — Exceptional**: Critical problem with a genuinely novel approach or new research direction; clear theory of change.

### 2. Execution Quality
*How sound are methodology, implementation, and findings?*
- **1 — Seriously flawed**: Methodology broken, results uninterpretable, or implementation doesn't work.
- **2 — Weak**: Significant gaps — missing validation, flawed design, or incomplete implementation.
- **3 — Competent**: Technically solid given the short duration; interpretable results; limitations acknowledged.
- **4 — Strong**: Thorough methodology, convincing validation; results clearly support conclusions.
- **5 — Exceptional**: Ambitious scope executed rigorously; surprising findings, novel methods, or robust validation.

### 3. Presentation & Clarity
*How clearly are the work, findings, and impact communicated?*
- **1 — Incomprehensible**: Cannot tell what the project claims or does.
- **2 — Hard to follow**: Key information buried, missing, or diluted by excess length.
- **3 — Clear enough**: Problem, approach, results and limitations are understandable without undue effort.
- **4 — Well presented**: Easy to follow, well structured, appropriate detail.
- **5 — Exceptionally clear**: Complex ideas made accessible; could serve as a model.

Other judging facts:
- A judge may decline to score a report too unclear to follow.
- Judges typically take about a week after the event to score.
- Individual scores aren't shared (the rubric itself is public). Feedback is sent to participants without reviewer names.
- Results are typically announced 1–2 weeks after the judging deadline. Winners are contacted directly; all participants get reviewer feedback by email.

## AI Tool Use, Prior Work, and Disqualification

- AI tools are allowed, "however they help you." The team is responsible for everything in the report.
- **Made-up results or references can disqualify a project.**
- Building on existing/prior research is allowed and encouraged — but the team must clearly identify what was done during the sprint itself.
- **Undisclosed prior work can lead to disqualification.** Code written before the sprint must be marked as preexisting in both the README and the report.
- No separate plagiarism clause is stated on this page.
- No stated citation-style requirement.

## Submission Process

- Submit via the official submission form on the sprint page.
- **Deadline: Sunday, November 15, 2026, 11:59 PM AoE (Anywhere on Earth)** — equivalently Nov 16, 11:59 AM UTC. All schedule times on the page are given in UTC.
- Resubmission: submit again using the **same title** before the deadline; the new submission **replaces** the old one. Do not email the PDF instead.
- Confirmation: a confirmation email (with project title + PDF) arrives shortly after submitting — check it, and check spam if it doesn't arrive, then email sprints@apartresearch.com.
- Visibility: projects do **not** appear on the website until after judging, "due to the risk of infohazards." The confirmation email is your proof of submission.
- Unfinished work is explicitly allowed; honest limitations are welcome.

## FAQ (paraphrased, grouped as on the page)

**Getting started**
- *How the sprint works*: sign up → join Discord (https://discord.gg/XswWBvugYs) → form/join a team or go solo → pick a track and question → build → submit a PDF by the deadline.
- *Duration*: three days, Nov 13–15, 2026; deadline end of day Sunday Nov 15, 11:59 PM AoE.

- *Team formation*: before/during the sprint via Discord; solo is fine; up to 5 recommended.
- *Tracks and scoring*: all projects use the same rubric regardless of track.

- *Talks*: recorded and shared on Discord and the Schedule tab.
- *Timezone*: AoE for the deadline.
- *Attendance*: optional/recommended, not required; the Sunday cutoff is the only hard deadline.
- *Eligibility*: global.

**Submissions**
- *What to submit*: a PDF research report using the official template — "a mini research paper, not a product demo" — including the Limitations/Dual-Use appendix.

- *Fixing/updating*: resubmit with same title before deadline; don't email it; ask @Support on Discord if unsure.
- *Adding team members*: resubmit with same title, full team list, and PDF before deadline.
- *Unfinished work*: allowed.
- *AI tools*: allowed (same rule as above).
- *Building on existing research*: allowed with disclosure (same rule as above).
- *Multiple projects*: up to two per person, each its own submission with a unique title.
- *Citation style*: no requirement.

**Judging and results**
- *How judging works*: experts review the PDF and score it on the rubric, about a week after the event.
- *Judge scores*: not shared individually.
- *Results timing*: typically 1–2 weeks after the judging deadline.
- *Fellowship*: top teams fast-tracked for the Apart Fellowship — accelerated consideration, not a guaranteed place; the fellowship runs its own separate review.

**Support**
- Discord help-desk channel, tag `@Support`, or email sprints@apartresearch.com.
