# Design Sketches for a More Sensible World

**Authors:** Owen Cotton-Barratt, Lizka Vaintrob, Oly Sourbut, Rose Hadshar
**Source:** Forethought (research organization), 5 February 2026
**URL:** https://www.forethought.org/research/design-sketches-for-a-more-sensible-world
**Accessed:** 2026-11-14
**Type:** Expert analysis (institutional think-tank publication; introduction to a four-part article series, not peer-reviewed)

## Summary

This is the introductory article to a four-part Forethought series proposing concrete technology concepts ("design sketches") meant to help society navigate a period of fast, poorly-understood AI-driven change. The authors argue that humanity is currently "muddling through" AI progress without adequate foresight, and that survival under present conditions would owe more to luck than wisdom. They sketch categories of AI-enabled tools intended to improve collective epistemics, individual decision-making, strategic awareness, and group coordination, explicitly framing these as early, illustrative ideas rather than finished designs or forecasts.

## Key Claims / Findings

- Central premise: society does not understand what it is doing with respect to AI progress, and foresight is not keeping pace with acceleration. The authors compare this to stepping off a spaceship onto an unknown planet, where survival would mean "you were lucky, not... wise."
- The authors propose a "more sensible world" in which people recognize the limits of their own understanding and build AI-based tools to support reasoning and coordination through the transition, and argue such a world is achievable in the near term.
- They explicitly caveat that the sketches are early-stage and that the technologies that eventually prove useful "may look quite different" from what is described.
- The piece organizes nineteen proposed tool concepts into four clusters:
  - **Collective epistemics** (5 sketches) — tools for identifying trustworthy information and rewarding honesty: community notes for everything, rhetoric highlighting, reliability tracking, epistemic virtue evals, provenance tracing.
  - **Angels-on-the-shoulder** (5 sketches) — personalized tools narrowing the gap between how well people could decide and how they actually decide: aligned recommender systems, personalised learning systems, automated deep briefings, reflection scaffolding, guardian angels.
  - **Tools for strategic awareness** (3 sketches) — tools deepening understanding of what is happening in the world: ambient superforecasting, scenario planning on tap, automated OSINT.
  - **Defense-favoured coordination tech** (6 sketches) — tools making coordination, dispute resolution, and deal-making faster and cheaper: fast facilitation, automated negotiation, arbitrarily easy arbitration, a "magic network" for connecting relevant parties, structured transparency for democratic oversight, and confidential monitoring/verification.
- On the coordination cluster, the authors explicitly flag dual-use risk — the same technology could enable collusion or criminality — and say they deliberately favor designs that advantage defense over offense.
- The introduction frames a causal chain: augmented decision-making and improved coordination feed better public discourse and shared understanding, which in turn reduce mistakes and conflict and increase trust, cumulatively yielding a "more sensible world."
- The authors acknowledge this world would still face serious risks (e.g., deep value disagreements, very rapid progress) but argue it would be better positioned to notice and respond to them.
- The piece discloses that the authors used AI assistance in preparing the article and thanks roughly twenty reviewers.

## Relevance to the sprint

This piece is directly on-theme for "AI x Epistemics": it is a named sprint-wide reading that catalogs concrete product concepts (community notes, reliability tracking, provenance tracing, epistemic virtue evals) aimed squarely at trust infrastructure and models' epistemic behavior, several of which map onto plausible hackathon build targets. Its "defense-favoured coordination tech" cluster also raises the dual-use/adoption question explicitly, which is useful for projects thinking about real-world deployment risk rather than just technical feasibility.
